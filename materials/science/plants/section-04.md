## What we learned

Plants cannot move, so they use clever methods to spread their seeds away from the parent plant. This helps reduce competition **for light, water, and nutrients**.

We also made paper helicopters (like sycamore seeds!) — to see **wind dispersal** in action.

## Vocabulary

| Word              | Meaning                                                |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Seed dispersal    | How seeds are spread away from the parent plant        |
| Wind dispersal    | Seeds carried by the air                               |
| Animal dispersal  | Seeds eaten or carried by animals                      |
| Water dispersal   | Seeds that float                                       |
| Explosion         | Pods burst open and scatter seeds                      |

## Topic notes

### Why plants need to disperse their seeds
- To avoid overcrowding
- To find space
- To get enough sunlight
- To reduce competition with the parent plant
- To spread to new areas

### Types of seed dispersal

**1. Wind dispersal** — Seeds are light and often have wings or fluff.
*Examples:* Dandelions, Sycamore "helicopters", Ash seeds.

**2. Animal dispersal** — Animals help spread seeds by eating fruits and dropping the seeds later, or by carrying seeds that stick to their fur.
*Examples:* Blackberries, Burdock burrs, Acorns.

**3. Water dispersal** — Seeds float and travel along rivers or the sea.
*Examples:* Coconut and Mangrove seeds.

**4. Explosion** — Seed pods dry out and burst open suddenly. **BOOM!**
*Examples:* Peas, Some Fungi, Poppies.
