## What we learned

We explored different fruits to find out what seeds look like and how they are arranged inside. We cut open fruits, counted the seeds, and compared them (apple, tomato, mange tout or a pepper, and a kiwi).

You did a brilliant job noticing patterns — like how some fruits have one big seed (like a peach), and others have many small seeds (like a tomato).

## Vocabulary

| Word  | Meaning                                  |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Fruit | The part of a plant that contains seeds  |
| Seed  | The part that grows into a new plant     |
| Flesh | The soft part of the fruit               |
| Stone | A single large seed (e.g., peach)        |

## What we did

- Cut open different fruits (apple, tomato, kiwi, pepper, mange tout)
- Counted the seeds inside
- Drew diagrams of the fruits and labelled the seeds
- Talked about why fruits have seeds and what seeds do

> *Visual on page:* two grouped illustrations — **Fruits with many seeds** (tomato, kiwi, pepper, etc.) and **Fruits with one seed** (peach, plum, mango, etc.).

## Original questions (Try It)

1. Why do fruits contain seeds?
2. Which fruit had the most seeds?
3. Which fruit has only one seed?
4. What did you notice about where seeds are found?
