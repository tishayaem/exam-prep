# Brighton College 11+ — the interview (and assessment day)

Research to prepare a Year 5 child for the **interview stage** of Brighton College entry at 11+ (Year 7). Covers the admissions process, the interview itself, a categorised bank of likely questions, what interviewers reward and penalise, how to prepare a 10-year-old without over-coaching, the on-the-day written tasks (creative writing + maths), and how the school's own ethos should shape honest "why Brighton?" answers.

> **Method note.** Assembled by a five-angle fan-out web-research pass with confidence-flagging, then a verification pass on the load-bearing claims. **Caveat that colours everything below:** Brighton College's own website, the Good Schools Guide and the main tutoring sites all **block automated fetching (HTTP 403)** — so wording attributed to the school's pages comes via *search-engine extraction* of those exact pages, not a byte-for-byte read. The core *facts* (process, two interviews, the three values) are corroborated across five independent passes and many sources, so the **substance is High confidence even where the exact phrasing isn't byte-verified**. Where a "fact" is really tutoring-industry convention rather than something Brighton publishes, it's flagged — don't present those to a child as gospel.

## ⚠️ Action item #0 — confirm three things with Admissions

A five-minute email/call to Brighton College admissions resolves the genuine ambiguities the public sources can't:

1. **11+ format: the school's own papers, or ISEB?** The school's own pages describe an **in-house Academic Assessment Day in December** (maths, English, verbal & non-verbal reasoning) — **not** the ISEB Common Pre-Test. Several tutoring sites say "ISEB at 11+", but that appears to be **the 13+ route bleeding in, or out of date.** This matters: the rest of *this* app is framed around "ISEB written Common Entrance", which looks like a **mismatch for Brighton 11+**. Confirm which papers your child actually sits.
2. **Are the maths/written papers multiple-choice or write-in (show your working)?** Sources conflict (the MCQ timings that circulate are the hallmark of the 13+ ISEB pre-test). An MCQ paper and a "show your working" paper are different beasts to prepare for.
3. **The interview logistics** — confirmed as *two short interviews with teaching staff* in January, but length and one-to-one-vs-pair are **not published**. Ask if you want certainty.

This mirrors "Action item #0" in the GL research README: the single phone call changes what's worth practising.

## 1. The Brighton College 11+ process (what the interview sits inside)

**11+ (Year 7 entry) — day pupils only. Two separate days. [High confidence — school's own pages, triangulated.]**

| Stage | When | What happens |
|-------|------|--------------|
| Register | 1 Aug – **30 Nov** (register early; ~Oct recommended) | Apply for a place |
| Taster Days | May / Sept / Nov | Optional; see the school |
| **Academic Assessment Day** | **early December** of Year 6 | Sit the school's papers: **Maths, English, Verbal Reasoning, Non-Verbal Reasoning** (all candidates auto-considered for academic scholarship) |
| **Interview & Activities Day** | **January** | **Immersive activities with teachers + TWO short interviews with members of teaching staff** |
| Offers | by **end of January** | — |

**Key disambiguation. [High.]** Brighton has many entry points (3+, 7+, 8+, 10+, **11+**, **13+**, 16+) and sources blur them:

- **11+ = the school's own December papers**, then the January interview/activities day. ~50–55 enter at 11+.
- **13+ = the ISEB Common Pre-Test** (online, adaptive, sat at the child's own prep school Oct–Dec of Year 6), then a **February Orientation Day** (activities + interviews + **written English & Maths**). ~180 enter at 13+ (the main intake; boarding starts here).
- **"Brighton College interview" results on Glassdoor are STAFF job interviews** — irrelevant to a child applicant. Ignore them.

**Selectivity. [High — Good Schools Guide.]** Competitive but not stratospheric: GSG reports a bar "just above 110" standardised, and the Head's line that he takes children "on the bright side of average" and it's "certainly easier to get into than a super-selective grammar." The school weighs the **whole picture** — papers + interview + fit — consistent with its character-first ethos (§7). Keystone's rule of thumb for selective schools generally: the written exam can be only **~one-third** of the decision, with interview and the current-school reference making up the rest — so **the interview genuinely matters.** [Medium — single reputable source.]

## 2. The interview itself

- **Format:** **Two short interviews** with **members of the teaching staff** (not solely the Head), on the January day. GSG frames it as "two meetings with a staff member [to] examine a pupil's character and potential." **[High.]**
- **One-to-one or panel?** Almost certainly individual (the *activities* are the group element), but this is **third-party inference, not an explicit school statement.** [Medium.]
- **Length:** "Short." No minutes published. (The "~15 min" figure online is attached to older/senior entry, not 11+.) [Low.]
- **Tone:** Reported "very relaxed and friendly… so tell them not to worry" (a Mumsnet parent) — a conversation, not an interrogation. [Low–Medium — anecdotal but consistent.]
- **What they're probing:** character, curiosity, motivation, and what the child would *contribute* (sport, music, drama, clubs) — i.e. "will this child be happy and thrive here, and add to the place?" [Medium as theme; aligns with the school's stated ethos.]

> **Honesty flag — there is no public bank of *real* Brighton 11+ interview questions.** The "Brighton College interview questions" lists online are either **tutor-*generated* practice sets** (e.g. BlackStone) or belong to **13+/Sixth-Form** entry (Student Room). Use the question bank in §4 for *familiarisation and practice talking* — not as "what they will ask." The most reliable intel is the school's own ethos (§7) plus the near-universal selective-school question patterns (§4), which are well-evidenced.

## 3. What interviewers reward — and the red flags

These come from across UK independent-admissions sources (Good Schools Guide, Keystone, Atom, Kidcoach, Bonas MacFarlane, Eleven Plus Exams, LWL). They are **selective-school convention, well-triangulated**, not Brighton-published — but they fit Brighton's curious/confident/kind ethos closely. [High as convention.]

**Rewarded:**
- **Genuine curiosity and visible enthusiasm — the headline quality.** A registrar quoted by GSG: *"I am looking for sparkly eyes and interest. If a child just sits there like a pudding, you usually don't take them."*
- **Thinking aloud / reasoning, not just right answers.** Kidcoach: *"It's not about knowing the answer, but… being able to express what you are thinking and how you would approach a situation."*
- **Being a contributor / team player** — eager to join in sport, music, drama, clubs.
- **Genuineness over polish** — *"honesty is the quality they're looking for"*; schools want *"curiosity, warmth and potential, not polished performances."*
- **Good manners and likeability** — a child who'll be *"a pleasure to teach."*
- **Saying "I don't know — but I'd guess…"** rather than freezing.

**Red flags:**
- **Over-rehearsed, coached-sounding answers — the #1 warning.** *"Interviewers can spot over-rehearsed children a mile off."* A fluent, perfectly-structured "why this school?" with no genuine specifics reads as coached.
- **One-word / monosyllabic answers** ("yes", "no") with no elaboration.
- **Parroting parents' or tutors' opinions** — *"say what's in their heart, not what their teacher told them to say."*
- **"I don't know" then dead silence.**
- **Poor body language** — fidgeting, slouching, leg-swinging, no eye contact, no greeting.
- **Arrogance** (the flip side of confidence) — "confident but not arrogant".

**The single most-repeated piece of advice, near-verbatim everywhere:** *be authentic; don't over-rehearse.* The best preparation is *"not interview coaching but broadening the child's experiences and giving them things to genuinely talk about."*

## 4. The question bank (for practice talking — not a script)

Categories ordered roughly by how reliably they appear. Each is well-attested across UK 11+/13+ sources. ★ = flagged "most common / near-universal".

### A. About yourself
- ★ "Tell me about yourself." / "How would you describe yourself?"
- "If your friends described you in three words, what would they be?" *(reported at Eton, Westminster, Habs, Whitgift, GDST schools)*
- "Tell me about your family — where you live, brothers and sisters."
- "What's your greatest achievement so far?" / "What's the biggest challenge you've faced?"

### B. Hobbies, interests, what you do outside school
- ★ "What do you like to do in your free time?" / "What do you enjoy outside the classroom?"
- "Tell me about your extra-curricular interests." / "What's your greatest achievement at [your sport/instrument]?"
- "Have you ever started a club or activity?" *(probes initiative — fits Brighton's 'contributor' lens)*
- Sometimes a **"show and tell"**: bring an object and explain why it matters to you.

### C. Reading & books — **the single most common question**
- ★★ "What are you reading at the moment? Tell me about it."
- "What kinds of books do you like?" / "Describe your favourite character — and how would you have ended the book differently?"
- Academic variant: read an **unseen passage/poem aloud** and answer comprehension questions on it.
- *Prep: always have a current book you can talk about — plot, a character, why you like it, and one thing you'd change.*

### D. Current affairs / the news
- ★ "Tell me about a news story that's interested you recently — and why."
- "Tell me something interesting you've learnt lately."
- "What's one thing we could actually do about climate change?"
- *Prep: read child-friendly news (First News, The Week Junior, Newsround). Know who the PM is, the party in government, the monarch, and one or two big world stories — with a **reason** for an opinion.*

### E. Hypothetical / lateral-thinking ("no right answer" — they want your reasoning)
- "If you were Prime Minister for the day, what would you do?"
- "Would you rather be able to fly or be invisible?" / "a clever coward or a brave fool?"
- "Describe a future invention and how it would change our lives."
- "If you could meet anyone, living or dead, who and why?"
- *Note: Oxbridge-style "how many windows in London?" estimation puzzles are **not** age-typical at 11+ — don't drill them.*

### F. "Why this school?" / motivation
- ★ "Why do you want to come to Brighton College?" / "Why does this school suit you?"
- "Have you visited? What stood out?"
- "What do you like about your current school? What would you change if you were head?"
- *Prep: pick 3–4 things that genuinely appeal (see §7) and tie each to a **real** interest of the child's. Specific + true beats fluent + generic.*

### G. Opinion / gentle ethics
- "Is being kind to other people important?" / "What makes a good friend?"
- "What makes someone a good member of their community?"
- *(More competitive schools push further: "Should all jobs be paid equally?" — handle with reasons, weighing two sides; there's no 'right' answer.)*

### H. The closer — **questions the child asks back** (expected!)
- ★ "Do you have any questions for us?" — an unprepared child gets thrown. **Have 1–2 genuine, specific questions ready**, e.g.:
  - "I really enjoy [subject/sport/instrument] — how does the school support that?"
  - "Is there a [specific club] / school council?"
- *Method: from the school website, turn 1–2 things that genuinely appeal into a question. Avoid anything answerable in one line off the homepage.*

## 5. How to answer well (techniques, not scripts)

- **Beat the one-word answer.** Aim for "more than one sentence": a point, then a reason or example. Gentle scaffolds exist — **PEE** (Point, Explanation, Evidence) for opinions; a **positive–negative–positive "hamburger"** for "what don't you like…?" — but treat them as *training wheels in practice and drop the visible formula on the day*, or it sounds canned.
- **The "I don't know" move — drill it until automatic.** Never stop at "I don't know." Add *"…but I'd guess…"* and reason aloud: *"I don't know much about that — I suppose I'd start by wondering… this reminds me of [something I do know]…"* Practise on deliberately unfamiliar questions so it becomes a reflex.
- **Pause before answering** — 3–5 seconds to think is fine and preferred to rushing. Don't feel hurried.
- **Have opinions, with reasons.** Build this at home: discuss something, then ask *"why do you think that?"* and gently push back.
- **Listen and take turns** — it's a conversation; answer, then listen to the follow-up rather than monologuing.

## 6. Manners & body language (rehearse the mechanics — this part *should* be practised)

- Smile; greet warmly; **firm handshake, eye contact, introduce yourself** (avoid the "wet, limp hand").
- **Wait to be invited to sit.**
- Sit upright, bottom to the back of the seat — this naturally kills fidgeting and leg-swinging.
- Polite greeting and goodbye; say thank you.

Everyone agrees: **prepare the mechanics and the familiarity** (manners, posture, pausing, knowing the format, researching the school, having real anecdotes ready) — and **don't memorise answer content.**

## 7. "Why Brighton College?" — the ethos to answer honestly against

An authentic answer draws on what genuinely resonates with the child from the school's *own* stated values — not a recital of all of them. [Values themselves: High — the school's own pages, triangulated.]

- **Three core values: "curious, confident and kind."** The contemporary strapline is *"Be Kind. Be Curious. Be Confident. Be the Best You."*
- **A culture of kindness is the centrepiece.** The school says it aims to "instil a culture of kindness… ensuring that everyone feels valued for who they truly are." The *Sunday Times* named it **Independent School of the Decade**, praising "the culture of kindness that underpins everything the school does."
- **Individuality is explicitly prized.** Head Master **Richard Cairns** (since 2006): "Children who are happy are children who thrive," and the school helps each child become "a first-class version of himself or herself, not the second-class version of someone else."
- **Intellectual curiosity + breadth.** It wants "well-educated, tolerant and intellectually curious young adults", with top-tier academics *plus* sport, the arts and community service. Tatler calls it "a masterclass in education" combining "an enlightened approach with a kindness-driven culture."

**How a child uses this (honestly):** pick the one or two that are *true* for them — e.g. "I love that the school is known for being kind, because at my school I…", or "I really want to keep doing [music/sport/science] and Brighton has…". A genuine, specific reason rooted in the child's real interests beats a polished list every time (see the over-rehearsal red flag, §3).

## 8. The on-the-day written tasks (creative writing + maths)

These sit on the **December Academic Assessment Day** (not the January interview day), but they're part of the same prep.

- **English — comprehension + a creative-writing piece.** That the English paper includes creative writing is asserted by tutoring sites; the school's own pages say only "English". **[Medium — third-party; not confirmed verbatim by the school.]**
- **What good writing is marked on** (verbatim wording, but tied to the **13+** Orientation-Day writing assessment — a reasonable proxy for 11+): marks for "imagination, creativity, form, purpose, organisation, cohesion and sentence structure", plus "spelling, vocabulary and punctuation". **[Medium; treat as indicative for 11+.]** Practical read: **imagination + structure + technical accuracy**, all three.
- **Creative-writing prompt style:** **not published.** Generic 11+ tasks use a title, an opening line, a theme, or a picture stimulus — but there is **no Brighton-specific confirmation** of which. [Low / not documented.]
- **Maths:** problem-solving + arithmetic; one tutoring source times it at ~50 minutes. Whether it's **multiple-choice or write-in is genuinely unclear** in public sources (MCQ timings online likely come from the 13+ ISEB pre-test). **Confirm with the school** (Action item #0) before optimising for MCQ-vs-working. [Low–Medium.]

## 9. A practical prep plan for a 10-year-old

1. **Daily habit, not cramming.** Discuss one thing from child-friendly news, then "why do you think that?" + gentle push-back. Builds opinions-with-reasons and the think-aloud reflex at once.
2. **Drill the "I don't know → but I'd guess…" move** until it's automatic.
3. **Keep a current book on the go** they can actually talk about (plot, a character, what they'd change).
4. **Rehearse only the mechanics** (§6): greeting, handshake, eye contact, wait to sit, sit up, pause, more-than-one-sentence.
5. **Research Brighton together** (§7) so "why us?" is specific and true; prepare 1–2 questions to ask back (§4H).
6. **One light, low-pressure mock** with an adult the child doesn't know well — to get used to talking to a stranger, *not* to script answers.
7. **Above all, let them be themselves.** Schools actively screen out the coached child and look for "sparkly eyes".
