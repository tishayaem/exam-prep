# 11+ (GL Assessment style) — research & exercise bank

Research compiled to support a Year 5 child preparing for a **written, GL Assessment–style 11+** at an independent UK school, sitting in roughly 6 months. Covers the three subjects asked for — **Maths, Verbal Reasoning (VR), Non-Verbal Reasoning (NVR)** — plus exam mechanics and a prep plan.

> Method note: this was assembled by a fan-out web-research pass with adversarial fact-checking, then two focused follow-up passes (one for NVR, one for Maths) that read GL's own free familiarisation booklets. Claims are cited. Where a "fact" is really a tutoring-industry convention rather than something GL officially publishes, that's flagged — don't present those to a child as gospel.

## ⚠️ Action item #0 — confirm the format with the actual school

Everything here assumes a **GL Assessment** style written paper because that's the most common standardised format and what you selected. But GL's own site warns: *"The exact timings for, and number of questions in, your local 11+ test may differ."* For an **independent** school specifically, the school may set a **bespoke in-house paper** rather than buy a stock GL paper. Before investing heavily in (say) cube-net and paper-folding drills, confirm with the school's admissions office:

1. Which subjects are tested, and is reasoning included at all?
2. GL paper, another provider, or the school's own paper?
3. Multiple-choice or write-in answers?
4. Timing and question count per paper?
5. Is the separate **Spatial Reasoning** paper used? (Only a few regions use it — Kent, Lincolnshire, Buckinghamshire.)

This single phone call changes what's worth practising. Treat the timings below as a planning baseline, not a guarantee.

## What a GL-style 11+ covers

GL papers test **four subjects**: **Verbal Reasoning, Non-Verbal Reasoning, English, and Maths**. GL confirms this with **six free familiarisation PDFs** (the four subjects + a combined NVR+Maths paper + a Verbal Skills paper). GL does **not** release real past papers — only familiarisation/sample material (~10 hours' worth); real practice packs are sold separately.
[GL free materials](https://11plus.gl-assessment.co.uk/pages/free-materials) · [11plusguide](https://www.11plusguide.com/11-plus-papers-books/free-11-plus-papers/)

## How GL scoring works — standardisation & age-weighting

This matters because it changes how you read practice-paper scores. A child's **raw** score is converted to a **Standard Age Score (SAS)** in two steps:

1. **Equal-weighting** each paper, so papers with different question counts/timings count equally.
2. **Age-standardisation**: adjusting for the child's age **in years and months**. Younger children in the cohort get a small uplift for the same raw score; **older children are never penalised**.

- Mean SAS ≈ **100**, standard deviation **15** (this is the part GL officially publishes).
- Typical reported range ≈ **69–70 (low) to 140–141 (high)** — but note **141 is a reported cut-off, not a true maximum**, and this exact range is a tutoring-industry convention, not an official GL figure.
- NFER's worked illustration: the same raw score of 100 can map to SAS 118 for an older child vs 121 for a younger one.

Practical takeaway: a summer-born / younger-in-year child gets a built-in fairness adjustment, so don't panic about raw scores in isolation. The differentiator the test is really chasing is **speed *and* accuracy under time pressure**.
[Standardised scores explained](https://www.elevenplusexams.co.uk/advice-preparation/advice/standardised-scores-an-explanation) · [GL guide to standardised tests](https://www.gl-assessment.co.uk/guides/a-guide-to-standardised-tests/) · [Teachitright](https://teachitright.com/how-is-the-gl-assessment-11-plus-scored-and-what-is-the-pass-mark/)

## Timing & pacing (planning baseline — confirm locally)

- Industry guidance is roughly **36–50 seconds per question** on GL papers; Maths is often cited as **~50 questions in 50 minutes** (≈1 min/question), NVR as **~80 questions in 4 timed sections of 20 (~60 min)**.
- **No calculators** in Maths.
- Pacing is itself a skill being assessed: the papers differentiate partly on who works **quickly and accurately** under time pressure. Teach "don't get stuck — mark it, move on, come back."

## A 6-month prep sequence (build skills first, time-pressure later)

The evidence-backed consensus (11plusguide's revision plan, Atom) is: **core skills first, timed papers layered in later.** Doing papers alone — or skills alone — plateaus a child. Over-doing full papers is counter-productive.

- **Months 1–3 (breadth & foundations).** Build core skill across all topics. Short, frequent sessions beat marathons. Teach each VR/NVR *type* explicitly (use the per-type worked examples in this folder), and shore up the Maths topics in `maths.md`, especially anything beyond standard Year-5 KS2 (reverse percentages, nth-term, polygon angles, four-quadrant coordinates). Mental-arithmetic fluency every day.
- **Months 4–5 (consolidation + introduce timing).** Keep building weak spots, but start **timed sections** so speed develops. Begin error-logging: every wrong answer gets revisited until it's secure (your app's Mistakes mode is exactly this idea).
- **Final ~6 weeks (exam conditions).** Roughly **one timed, exam-condition paper per subject per week** — quiet room, strict timer, halfway/10-min/5-min warnings. **Resist doing more** than that; volume past this point doesn't help and burns the child out. Focus the remaining energy on the recurring mistakes in the error log.

[11plusguide revision plan](https://www.11plusguide.com/11-plus-exam-preparation/11-plus-revision/eleven-plus-revision-plan/) · [Atom GL guide](https://www.atomlearning.com/blog/gl-11-plus-guide)

## Standing caveats (read before trusting any single number)

1. **"21 VR types" and the "69–141 score range" are tutoring conventions**, not official GL publications. GL publishes the mean=100/SD=15 model and familiarisation booklets — not a fixed type list or named range.
2. **GL's real papers bundle the verbose "types" into fewer answer-format families** (e.g. NVR ≈ analogies, series, matrices, odd-one-out, codes). The long type lists are teaching scaffolds.
3. **Format varies by region/school.** "50 Qs / 50 min, multiple-choice, no calculator" for Maths is the best-supported baseline, not a universal rule. Independent schools may use write-in answers and bespoke papers.
4. **Spatial Reasoning is a separate paper used in only a few regions** — don't drill cube-nets/paper-folding until you've confirmed it's on your child's exam.
5. **Many cited pages are tutoring/publisher sites** with a commercial incentive. GL's own site and CGP are the most authoritative; cross-check question counts against the school's admissions page.
6. **Links live as of mid-2026** but CDNs/aggregators can move files.
