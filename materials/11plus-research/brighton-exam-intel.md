## ⚠️ CORRECTION — 11 June 2026, direct from Brighton College admissions (outranks everything below)

**Brighton College 11+ is two-stage:**

1. **Stage 1: the ISEB Common Pre-Test.** Children sit it first — online,
   adaptive, multiple-choice (English, Maths, VR, NVR). Brighton accesses and
   reviews the results.
2. **Stage 2: the Academic Assessment Day** in December of Year 6 (the school's
   own papers), then the January Interview & Activities Day as previously
   documented.

**What this inverts in the report below:**

- *"No pre-test at 11+ / 11+ ≠ ISEB"* — stamped **High confidence** in the
  report and in `interview.md` — was **wrong**.
- **Atom Learning was right, not an erroneous outlier.** Its "11+ candidates sit
  the ISEB Common Pre-Test" claim, dismissed below as route-conflation, matched
  what admissions confirmed.
- **The circulating ISEB CPT timings are relevant after all** (commonly cited:
  English ~25 min, Maths ~50, VR ~36, NVR ~32 — verify against ISEB's official
  familiarisation materials before relying on them). They describe stage 1, not
  a 13+ red herring.
- The report's Recommendation 1 ("immediately correct any ISEB/MCQ-timing
  content in the app's Brighton track") is void; its opposite applies — the app
  **needs** an on-screen, timed, MCQ-paced ISEB track.

**What still stands from the report:**

- The December Academic Assessment Day exists and is school-set; the January
  Interview & Activities Day follows; offers by end of January [High].
- English on the written day = comprehension + creative writing [Medium —
  multiple tutoring sources + a parent account; composition of the day is now
  the main open question].
- The CSSE creative-writing band descriptors, the ranked mark-losers, the
  20-extract passage corpus, the calibration proxies (~80% / ~110 SAS), the
  taper guidance, and the activities-day behaviour evidence — all unaffected.

**The remaining open question for admissions:** what the December day's papers
consist of **now that ISEB already covers English, Maths, VR and NVR at stage
1** — still four written papers, or a narrower/different set (e.g. written
English + Maths only)? Also: the CPT sitting window and venue for Brighton
applicants.

**Methodology lesson (record for future passes):** a school website's *silence*
on ISEB was read as absence — but omission on a marketing-oriented admissions
page is not evidence of absence. And a school-partnered platform (Atom) was
discounted as a commercially-motivated outlier when such platforms often hold
*direct* format intel from schools so their mocks match. "School-published >
everything" fails when the school publishes selectively; the only true High is
the admissions office itself.

## The report as delivered (11 June 2026) — original confidence stamps preserved

*Prepared 11 June 2026. Covers the **11+ (Year 7, September 2027 entry)** route only — the school's own written papers, NOT the 13+ ISEB Common Pre-Test.* *(← this framing is the error the banner corrects)*

### TL;DR
- **The circulating MCQ timings (English 25 / Maths 50 / NVR 32 / VR 36 min) are the 13+ ISEB Common Pre-Test — NOT the 11+.** Brighton's 11+ is the school's *own* written papers sat on one Academic Assessment Day in December; the English paper includes a **written comprehension AND a creative-writing piece**, so working/extended writing is required, not pure multiple-choice. The exact 11+ timings and question counts are **not published** [High confidence the timings are misattributed; Medium on exact 11+ format]. *(corrected — see banner)*
- **The January Interview & Activities Day is genuinely under-documented**: Brighton publishes only "a range of fun activities with teachers" + "two short interviews." No public source details the tasks or scoring. Prepare to behaviour, not content.
- **For a ~110-SAS-bar school, "on track" ≈ consistently ~80%+ on age-appropriate timed practice and ~110+ standardised on familiarisation tests.** Taper practice in the final fortnight; the single biggest English differentiator at 10–11 is *controlled* writing (structure + accuracy), not floridity.

### 1. The Brighton 11+ papers
- **Format**: Four assessments — Maths, English, Verbal Reasoning, Non-Verbal Reasoning — sat on one Academic Assessment Day in early December of Year 6 [High; school-published]. English has two written components: a comprehension and a creative-writing task [Medium-High; multiple tutoring sources + a 2017 parent account].
- **The MCQ-timings trap**: Several sites (The Exam Coach's ISEB page, exampaperspractice.co.uk, 11plus-papers) quote "English 25, Maths 50, NVR 32, VR 36 minutes." These belong to the **ISEB Common Pre-Test used at 13+**, not the 11+ school papers. The Exam Coach's dedicated 11+ page explicitly lists 11+ "Exam length — Unknown" [High]. *(corrected — the CPT is stage 1 of 11+ too; the timings are relevant)*
- **No pre-test at 11+** *(WRONG — see banner)*: Owl Tutors' Brighton College admissions guide states: "Pupils wishing to attain a place in Year 7 (11+ route) will be invited to sit a series of academic tests in Maths, English, Verbal, and Non-verbal Reasoning in December of Year 6 … Pupils applying for a place in Year 9 (13+ route) … will sit the ISEB Pre-Test." Elsewhere Owl states plainly: "No pre-assessment or pre-testing used by Brighton College at 11+ … all places are awarded via one set of entrance exams and an activities and interview day" [Medium-High; reputable tutoring site, consistent with school]. **Atom Learning's Brighton College 11+ Entry Guide states verbatim: "All children applying for 11+ entry to Brighton College sit the ISEB Common Pre-Test … taken online and consists of multiple choice questions. It is entirely adaptive"** — the report judged this "directly contradicted by Brighton's own site … treat Atom's ISEB-at-11+ claim as an error [High this is wrong]". *(Atom was in fact correct — see banner.)*
- **Difficulty/style**: Maths is based on the KS2 / Year 5 National Curriculum, ranging "from arithmetic to multi-step problem solving" [High]. Selectivity is "just above 110" standardised (Good Schools Guide / Britannia-Study citing GSG: "standardised scores just above the national average (110) in VR, NVR, English and maths"). Intake size is confirmed by The Good Schools Guide review: "Around 50 arrive at 11+ from many independent and state schools, joining children from Brighton College Prep. Another 160 enter at 13+" [High; reputable independent review]. Head Richard Cairns historically described intake as "on the bright side of average" — i.e. selective but not super-selective grammar level [Low; single anecdote, Mumsnet 2016].
- **Format change for 2027?** No evidence Brighton is moving its 11+ to online/adaptive or adopting ISEB at 11+ *(it has — or always did at stage 1; see banner)*. Brighton's "How to Apply" page states Open Mornings are "held on one or two Saturdays each term," and for "2027 and 2028 entry: For pupils studying in the UK, please register by 1 October 2025" [High; school-published].

### 2. The January Interview & Activities Day
- **What's published** (the *only* authoritative description, Brighton website): "They'll take part in a range of fun activities with our teachers, and also have two short interviews with members of the teaching staff." Offers by end of January [High; school-published].
- **What's NOT public**: No source — school, press, tutoring, or forum — details the actual activities, interview length, 1:1 vs paired format, questions, or scoring rubric for the 11+ day [High that this is genuinely undocumented; caveat in addendum: forum search was partially blocked, so treat as "not found", not "absent"]. The clearest "two interviewers" first-hand account (The Student Room) is **Sixth Form (16+) entry**, not 11+ — do not rely on it for 11+.
- **Conflation warning**: The 13+ route has a *separate* "Orientation Day" in February with ISEB pre-test + written English/Maths. Do not confuse with the 11+ "Interview and Activities Day" in January [High].
- **Transferable evidence on observed group tasks** (from assessment-centre/education sources, labelled as comparable, not Brighton-specific): reads well — contributing ideas while listening, building on others ("yes, and…"), bringing in quieter children, calm assertiveness, volunteering for roles (timekeeper/note-taker); sinks a candidate — dominating/interrupting, aggression rather than assertiveness, total silence (giving assessors "nothing to assess"), ignoring the brief, free-riding [Medium; reputable but non-school sources, e.g. AssessmentDay: assessors are "not looking for the person who shouts the loudest, or talks the most"].
- **Inference, not fact**: Claims that "kindness/collaboration/leadership" are *scored* during the day are reasonable inferences from Brighton's "culture of kindness" branding (Sunday Times "UK School of the Decade"), but are **not** documented criteria [Low].

### 3. Top-band 11+ creative writing at 10–11
- **Published marking criteria exist**: The **CSSE (Consortium of Selective Schools in Essex) "11+ English Continuous Writing Sample Mark Scheme"** publishes four bands across five domains (Ideas, Vocabulary incl. spelling, Grammar, Structure, Punctuation). Top band (Band 4): "clear and original writing — may include abstract forms, philosophical ideas, metaphors"; "ambitious vocabulary used appropriately to enhance communication"; "securely structured … with a clear sense of direction and originality throughout"; "punctuation varied and used creatively." Band 2 by contrast: "no clear purpose in the writing: ideas listed," "basic vocabulary — ambitious words misapplied," "no paragraphs" [High; published by the exam consortium itself]. The Latymer School also publishes a "Creative Writing Mark Scheme" [Medium].
- **Weighting**: At independent schools, creative writing carries significant weight — tutoring convention puts it at roughly 30–50% of the English score [Medium; tutoring-industry convention, varies by school].
- **What separates a top script** (consistent across markers): a controlled narrative arc over a sprawling plot; a strong, crafted opening line; *showing* over *telling*; deliberate vocabulary (avoiding "nice/big/good"); sentence-length variety; accurate SPaG; an ending that doesn't fizzle. Top selective schools are "increasingly alert to over-tutored vocabulary and memorised metaphors" — a small, well-observed detail (e.g. "the way someone folds a newspaper") beats an elaborate simile [Medium; experienced tutors, corroborated across several incl. Creative Hare, Leading Tuition].
- **Highest-frequency mark-losers** (ranked from marker/tutor reports): (1) weak/rushed or "it was all a dream" endings; (2) no planning → no paragraphs/structure; (3) spelling and homophone errors (there/their, to/too, write/right); (4) over-long rambling sentences; (5) thesaurus-stuffed vocabulary misapplied; (6) ignoring the prompt's form/person/tense; (7) running out of time / too short [Medium; Bond published examiner-style commentaries + tutor consensus].
- **Realistic length** in ~25 minutes handwritten for an able 10-year-old: roughly 1–1.5 sides / 250–400 words of *quality* writing, with ~5 minutes spent planning and a final proofread [Medium; tutoring convention].

### 4. Passage corpus for hard comprehension practice
Selective independent English papers favour **pre-1950 classic prose** (the Alleyn's 11+ sample uses a *Great Expectations* extract; King's College School Wimbledon uses an atmospheric "derelict house" passage), plus some 20th-century children's classics and the occasional poem with technique questions. Recommended public-domain extracts at the right stretch (all out of UK copyright):

1. **Charles Dickens, *Great Expectations* (1861), ch.1** — "A fearful man, all in coarse grey…" (Magwitch on the marshes). Inference of character/atmosphere; vocabulary landmines: "coarse," "lamed," "briars."
2. **Charles Dickens, *Oliver Twist* (1838), ch.2** — "Please, sir, I want some more." Tone, irony, social setting.
3. **Frances Hodgson Burnett, *The Secret Garden* (1911), ch.1** — Mary Lennox introduced. Character inference; "disagreeable," "tyrannical."
4. **Frances Hodgson Burnett, *A Little Princess* (1905), opening** — Sara Crewe arriving in London. Mood, contrast.
5. **Kenneth Grahame, *The Wind in the Willows* (1908), ch.1** — "The Mole had been working very hard all the morning…" Personification, spring imagery; "whitewash," "imperiously."
6. **E. Nesbit, *Five Children and It* (1902), ch.1** — discovery of the Psammead. Dialogue, humour, the fantastic.
7. **E. Nesbit, *The Railway Children* (1906), ch.1** — the family's change of fortune. Inference of unspoken adult worry.
8. **Robert Louis Stevenson, *Treasure Island* (1883), ch.1** — "the brown old seaman… came plodding to the inn door." Description, menace; "capstan," "sabre cut."
9. **Lewis Carroll, *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* (1865), ch.1** — down the rabbit-hole. Nonsense logic, voice.
10. **Mark Twain, *The Adventures of Tom Sawyer* (1876), the whitewashing scene** — irony, persuasion.
11. **H.G. Wells, *The War of the Worlds* (1898), bk.1 ch.1** — the opening "intelligences greater than man's…" Non-fiction-style register, foreboding.
12. **Anna Sewell, *Black Beauty* (1877), ch.1** — first-person animal narrator. Empathy, viewpoint.
13. **Charles Dickens, *A Christmas Carol* (1843), stave 1** — "Marley was dead, to begin with." Voice, simile ("dead as a door-nail").
14. **Rudyard Kipling, *The Jungle Book* (1894), "Mowgli's Brothers"** — Shere Khan at the cave. Tension, animal hierarchy.
15. **L. Frank Baum, *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz* (1900), ch.1** — the cyclone; Kansas greyness. Setting, contrast.
16. **Poetry — Robert Frost, "The Road Not Taken" (1916)** — metaphor, choice; technique Qs on imagery/tone.
17. **Poetry — Alfred, Lord Tennyson, "The Eagle" (1851)** — compact imagery, personification; "crag," "azure."
18. **Poetry — Christina Rossetti, "Who Has Seen the Wind?" (1872)** — simple but rich for sound devices.
19. **Poetry — William Wordsworth, "I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud" (1807)** — imagery, simile, mood.
20. **Non-fiction (pre-1950) — extract from Charles Darwin's *The Voyage of the Beagle* (1839)** — observational register, technical vocabulary.

### 5. Calibration for the final 6 months (June → December)
- **Proxies for "on track"**: For a ~110-SAS school, aim for consistent **~80%+** on age-appropriate timed practice. Exam Papers Plus's Brighton College 11+ guide states: "Pupils who are able to score 80%+ consistently in our 11+ practice tests should be on track to pass the Brighton College 11+ test … this has historically been a fairly strong indicator" [Medium; tutoring-industry convention]. On standardised platforms, target **~110+ standardised** (roughly 75th percentile / stanine 7+). Atom gives a standardised age score and benchmarks against other applicants to the same school [Medium; platform-published].
- **In-person mock providers (Sussex/Surrey/London, autumn 2026)** — names confirmed as running 11+ mocks; dates/prices to confirm on their sites:
  - **SGS PTA mock tests** (Sutton Grammar School site, Surrey) — hall-based and online familiarisation tests for Year 5; `sgsptamocktests.com` *(addendum: 2026 booking confirmed open)*
  - **Eleven Plus Exams mock series** (`mocks.elevenplusexams.co.uk`) — GL-style multi-section mocks, paper sets A–F, standardised reports *(addendum: MCQ-format — now a good match for CPT stage-1 pacing)*
  - **Pillai Tutors** (Surrey/London) — mix of MCQ + standard-format English/Maths mocks for independents, Oct–Dec, marked with ranking and feedback; `pillaitutors.co.uk`
  - **11 Plus Tutoring** (online, Surrey-focused) — `11plustutoring.uk`
- **Evidence-based taper**: Build practice through Sept–Oct; peak full-length timed mocks in late Oct/Nov; in the **final fortnight reduce workload, introduce nothing new, and protect sleep** — Exam Papers Plus's own parent guide says "Reduce workload in the week before exams to avoid burnout, and don't introduce anything new at this stage." Cross-domain study-science consensus: shorter focused sessions beat marathons; one full rest day per week; a planned downshift before exam day, not a collapse [Medium-High; tutoring guide + broad study-science consensus].

### Standing Caveats (original)
- Brighton's own site and several tutoring sites block automated fetching; some school wording here is **snippet-derived**, not byte-read.
- Tutoring sources are commercially motivated; none is used as the sole basis for a High-confidence claim. *(The failure mode turned out to be the reverse: a commercially-partnered source was wrongly discounted.)*
- The 11+/13+ conflation is pervasive online — every timing/format claim was checked for which entry point it describes. *(The check itself embedded the false premise that ISEB ≠ 11+.)*
- Creative-writing weightings and length norms are tutoring conventions, not Brighton-published.
- The CSSE band descriptors are from an Essex grammar consortium, used here as the best *published* proxy; Brighton's own rubric is not public.
