## ⚠️ CORRECTION — 11 June 2026, direct from Brighton College admissions (outranks everything below)

**Brighton College 11+ is two-stage:**

1. **Stage 1: the ISEB Common Pre-Test.** Children sit it first — online,
   adaptive, multiple-choice (English, Maths, VR, NVR). Brighton accesses and
   reviews the results.
2. **Stage 2: the Academic Assessment Day** in December of Year 6 (the school's
   own papers), then the January Interview & Activities Day as previously
   documented.

**What this inverts in the report below:**

- *"No pre-test at 11+ / 11+ ≠ ISEB"* — stamped **High confidence** in the
  report and in `interview.md` — was **wrong**.
- **Atom Learning was right, not an erroneous outlier.** Its "11+ candidates sit
  the ISEB Common Pre-Test" claim, dismissed below as route-conflation, matched
  what admissions confirmed.
- **The circulating ISEB CPT timings are relevant after all** — but see the
  format scout at the end of this file: the test was revised, and the
  commonly circulated numbers describe the old version.
- The report's Recommendation 1 ("immediately correct any ISEB/MCQ-timing
  content in the app's Brighton track") is void; its opposite applies — the app
  **needs** an on-screen, timed, MCQ-paced ISEB track.

**What still stands from the report:**

- The December Academic Assessment Day exists and is school-set; the January
  Interview & Activities Day follows; offers by end of January [High].
- English on the written day = comprehension + creative writing [Medium —
  multiple tutoring sources + a parent account; composition of the day is now
  the main open question].
- The CSSE creative-writing band descriptors, the ranked mark-losers, the
  20-extract passage corpus, the calibration proxies (~80% / ~110 SAS), the
  taper guidance, and the activities-day behaviour evidence — all unaffected.

**The remaining open question for admissions:** what the December day's papers
consist of **now that ISEB already covers English, Maths, VR and NVR at stage
1** — still four written papers, or a narrower/different set (e.g. written
English + Maths only)? Also: the CPT sitting window and venue for Brighton
applicants.

**Methodology lesson (record for future passes):** a school website's *silence*
on ISEB was read as absence — but omission on a marketing-oriented admissions
page is not evidence of absence. And a school-partnered platform (Atom) was
discounted as a commercially-motivated outlier when such platforms often hold
*direct* format intel from schools so their mocks match. "School-published >
everything" fails when the school publishes selectively; the only true High is
the admissions office itself.

### 3. Top-band 11+ creative writing at 10–11
- **Published marking criteria exist**: The **CSSE (Consortium of Selective Schools in Essex) "11+ English Continuous Writing Sample Mark Scheme"** publishes four bands across five domains (Ideas, Vocabulary incl. spelling, Grammar, Structure, Punctuation). Top band (Band 4): "clear and original writing — may include abstract forms, philosophical ideas, metaphors"; "ambitious vocabulary used appropriately to enhance communication"; "securely structured … with a clear sense of direction and originality throughout"; "punctuation varied and used creatively." Band 2 by contrast: "no clear purpose in the writing: ideas listed," "basic vocabulary — ambitious words misapplied," "no paragraphs" [High; published by the exam consortium itself]. The Latymer School also publishes a "Creative Writing Mark Scheme" [Medium].
- **Weighting**: At independent schools, creative writing carries significant weight — tutoring convention puts it at roughly 30–50% of the English score [Medium; tutoring-industry convention, varies by school].
- **What separates a top script** (consistent across markers): a controlled narrative arc over a sprawling plot; a strong, crafted opening line; *showing* over *telling*; deliberate vocabulary (avoiding "nice/big/good"); sentence-length variety; accurate SPaG; an ending that doesn't fizzle. Top selective schools are "increasingly alert to over-tutored vocabulary and memorised metaphors" — a small, well-observed detail (e.g. "the way someone folds a newspaper") beats an elaborate simile [Medium; experienced tutors, corroborated across several incl. Creative Hare, Leading Tuition].
- **Highest-frequency mark-losers** (ranked from marker/tutor reports): (1) weak/rushed or "it was all a dream" endings; (2) no planning → no paragraphs/structure; (3) spelling and homophone errors (there/their, to/too, write/right); (4) over-long rambling sentences; (5) thesaurus-stuffed vocabulary misapplied; (6) ignoring the prompt's form/person/tense; (7) running out of time / too short [Medium; Bond published examiner-style commentaries + tutor consensus].
- **Realistic length** in ~25 minutes handwritten for an able 10-year-old: roughly 1–1.5 sides / 250–400 words of *quality* writing, with ~5 minutes spent planning and a final proofread [Medium; tutoring convention].

## ISEB CPT current format — direct scout (11 June 2026)

A three-call check of primary sources, prompted by the roadmap's
"verify the timings" note. It overturned them.

**Current format (2025–26 cycle) [High — ISEB's own Common Pre-Tests
support portal, corroborated by multiple 2026 guides]:**

| Subtest | Time |
|---------|------|
| English (reading comprehension + grammar) | **40 min** |
| Mathematics (NC-aligned, core + reasoning) | **40 min** |
| Non-Verbal Reasoning | **30 min** |
| Verbal Reasoning | **25 min** |

- ~2h15 total (+25% extra time where eligible); the four subtests can be
  sat together (short breaks between) or **split across sittings**.
- Adaptive difficulty; **all questions are marked** — no unscored/sample
  items in the live test.
- Runs on the **Century Tech platform** for 2025–26; adjustable text size,
  colour overlays and an **optional visible timer**.
